3 · Virtual Office 101

A registered address, without the real estate.

A Virtual Office is a registered business address that lets a business complete company incorporation, GST registration, official communication and documentation — without owning or renting physical office space.

What they get

What they get

What they get
Cost savings

How to explain it simply. Imagine running a tiffin business from home, but your visiting card shows a proper Connaught Place address. Clients trust you more, and you never pay office rent. That is exactly what a Virtual Office does.
3.1 · Why the address matters

Commercial vs residential.

A business address is not just a location. It decides whether government verification goes smoothly and whether customers take the business seriously.

Commercial address
  • Data security. Business data stays protected and private.
  • Lower personal exposure. Reduces risk to the owner’s home.
  • Improved trust. Clients and partners trust a business address.
  • More credibility. Projects a professional, established image.
Residential address
  • Data exposure. Personal address becomes publicly visible.
  • Security concerns. Home address linked to business records.
  • Lower trust. Customers may question legitimacy.
  • Lower credibility. Perceived as less established.
The cost of getting it wrong. A poor or incorrect address causes delays in registration, document rejection and a perception of being unprofessional — all expensive to undo.

6 · Registration concepts

What you are actually helping them do.

You do not file these for the client, but you will be asked about them on nearly every call. Knowing the basics is the difference between a confident answer and a lost deal.

Company registration · mca.gov.in

Legally creating the business under Indian law. The company becomes a separate legal ‘person’ — it can own things, sign contracts, and the owner’s personal assets are protected if the business faces losses.

  1. Choose structure — Pvt. Ltd., LLP, OPC
  2. Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)
  3. Director Identification Number (DIN)
  4. RUN form for name approval
  5. SPICe form with MOA & AOA
  6. Certificate of Incorporation from RoC
GST registration · gst.gov.in

A tax ID for the business. Mandatory above ₹20 lakh turnover (₹10 lakh for North-Eastern states), for inter-state trade and all e-commerce. Allows claiming Input Tax Credit.

  1. Create a user ID on gst.gov.in
  2. Fill form GST REG-01
  3. Upload PAN, Aadhaar, address proof, bank details
  4. Receive ARN (tracking number)
  5. Receive GSTIN on verification
The third concept — mailing address. The address on the website, letterhead, visiting card and Google Business profile. It does not mean the client works there, and it is what the Business Address plan serves.
7 · The document chain

Landlord → operator → client.

This chain is what legally establishes a client’s right to use a commercial address. Understanding it explains why certain documents exist, and why some spaces need more of them.

Link 1
Landlord
Owns the property. Provides the lease or rent agreement, ownership proof, and an NOC where applicable.
Link 2
Space operator
Leases, manages and provides the workspace. Issues the agreement, authorisation and address documents to the client.
Link 3
The client’s business
Uses the address for registration, compliance and correspondence.

Owned vs leased. For an owned space, documents come directly from the space. For a leased space, the operator must first have the building owner’s permission — requiring a Master Agreement and Master NOC to prove the chain.

Guide towards owned spaces. They are more stable, have lower rejection rates and need fewer supporting documents. Where the client has flexibility on location, steer them there.
7.1 · The NOC

The document that applications fail without.

A No Objection Certificate is a letter from the property owner or operator saying: “I am okay with you using my property for your business registration.”

Why it matters

Without an NOC, government authorities will reject the company or GST registration application. It is one of the most critical documents in the Virtual Office package, and it must be properly signed and stamped.

Think of it as a landlord writing a letter confirming the tenant is allowed to run a business from the premises.

What spaces provide
  • Space–client agreement
  • NOC to client
  • Utility bill — proof the address is real and active
  • Master Agreement & Master NOC (leased spaces only)
  • Landlord KYC, board resolution, property tax receipt
Missing or incorrectly formatted NOCs are a leading cause of government application rejections. The full plan-by-plan document matrix is covered in the Sales Pitch chapter.

02

What a Virtual Office is

The product, in plain terms

A Virtual Office is a registered business address that enables businesses to complete company incorporation, GST registration, official communication and documentation — without owning or renting physical office space. It is the infrastructure of legitimacy without the overhead of real estate.

The client gets three things: a professional address they can use on business documents and government filings; mail handling, where letters and packages are received and stored safely at the registered space; and cost savings, at a fraction of the cost of renting — no rent, no deposit, no overhead.

How to explain it simply. Imagine someone runs a tiffin business from home, but their visiting card shows a proper Connaught Place office address. Clients trust them more, yet they never pay rent for a physical office. That is exactly what a Virtual Office does.

Why the address matters

A business address is not just a location. A strong, compliant commercial address helps a company complete government verification smoothly, build trust with customers and partners, show professionalism and legitimacy, and create a credible market presence. A poor or incorrect address does the opposite: delays in registration, document rejection, low credibility with customers, and the perception of being unprofessional.

 Commercial addressResidential address
DataBusiness data stays protected and privatePersonal address becomes publicly visible
SecurityReduces personal exposure and riskHome address linked to business records
TrustClients and partners trust a business addressCustomers may question legitimacy
CredibilityProjects a professional, established imagePerceived as less established or serious

The three core use cases

Almost every Virtual Office purchase in India traces back to one of three needs, and identifying which one the client has is what determines the right plan.

Company registration. Government rules require a commercial address to legally register a company in India — a home or non-commercial address is not permitted under MCA guidelines. The output is a Certificate of Incorporation (COI).

GST registration. To obtain a GST number — mandatory for businesses with turnover above ₹20 lakh, or ₹10 lakh in North-Eastern states — a commercial address is required. The output is a GSTIN.

Official correspondence. A premium address for the website, visiting cards, letterheads and Google Business Profile, which builds immediate trust with clients and partners.

Other accepted registrations

Beyond company and GST registration, a Virtual Office address is accepted for a range of other government licences and registrations: Shops & Establishment certificate, Trust / NGO / charity setup, FSSAI licence (food businesses), RERA licence (real estate agents), APOB (Additional Place of Business, for a registered address in another city) and VPOB (Virtual Place of Business, for a compliant GST address without a physical office).

Verify before you promise. Many partner spaces do not provide documents for these additional use cases. Always confirm with the space partner before committing to a client.

Who buys it

Entrepreneurs and startups starting in a new city with no commercial address, who need compliance without expensive rent. Large and expanding companies entering new states, where each state requires its own GST registration and a Virtual Office avoids a physical branch. Freelancers and professionals working from home who need a professional address for client trust while keeping their home private. International companies entering India, who need a local commercial address to get compliant and start trading. Home-based and seasonal businesses, where a home address on invoices reduces client confidence and flexibility matters.

Registration concepts

What the client is actually trying to do

You do not file these on the client’s behalf, but you will be asked about them on nearly every call. Knowing the basics is the difference between a confident answer and a lost deal.

Company registration

Company registration is the process of legally creating a business under Indian law through the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. It is like giving the business a birth certificate: after it, the company officially exists in the eyes of the law as a separate legal ‘person’ that can own things, sign contracts, and shield the owner’s personal assets if the business faces losses or lawsuits.

The benefits a client cares about are legal protection for personal assets, credibility with clients, banks and investors, easier access to funding, and eligibility for various government tax deductions.

The process on mca.gov.in: choose the business structure (Pvt. Ltd., LLP, OPC and so on), obtain a Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for online filing, obtain a Director Identification Number (DIN) for all directors, file the RUN form for name approval, then the SPICe form with MOA and AOA, and finally receive the Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of Companies.

GST registration

GST registration is how a business gets officially recognised as a tax-paying entity — effectively a tax ID card for the company. It is mandatory if annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for North-Eastern states), and required for inter-state trade and all e-commerce businesses. Registration allows the business to claim Input Tax Credit on its purchases.

The process on gst.gov.in: create a user ID, fill form GST REG-01 with business details, upload documents (PAN, Aadhaar, address proof, bank details), receive an ARN as a tracking number after submission, and receive the GSTIN on successful verification.

Mailing address and official correspondence

The third concept is the simplest. A business’s official correspondence address is the one that appears on its website, letterhead, visiting card and Google Business profile. It does not imply the business operates from there. Clients, banks and suppliers send important letters to it, and with a Business Address plan that premium address belongs to the client — all letters and packages are received and stored safely at the space, for collection in person or courier delivery on request.

The document chain

Why the paperwork exists

The document chain is what legally establishes a client’s right to use a commercial address. It runs in three links: the landlord, who owns the property; the space operator, who leases, manages and provides the workspace; and the client’s business, which uses the address for registration, compliance and correspondence.

From landlord to operator flow the lease or rent agreement, ownership and property proof, an NOC where applicable, and other supporting property documents. From operator to client flow the agreement or service contract, authorisation to use the address, address-related documents and business-specific documentation.

Owned versus leased space

An owned space means the coworking operator owns the building. A leased space means they rent it from someone else. The distinction matters because the documents required for a Virtual Office application differ: for an owned space, documents come directly from the space; for a leased space, the operator must first have obtained the building owner’s permission, which requires a Master Agreement and a Master NOC to prove the chain of permission.

Recommendation: owned spaces are preferred. They are more stable, have lower rejection rates and require fewer supporting documents. When a client has flexibility on location, guide them towards owned-space options.

What spaces provide

A space–client agreement is the legal contract authorising the client to use the address. The NOC grants official permission to use the address for government registration. A utility bill — electricity or water — confirms the physical address is real and active. For leased spaces only, a Master Agreement establishes the operator’s right to sublease and a Master NOC records the building owner’s permission to sublease to multiple clients. Supporting documents such as landlord KYC, board resolution and property tax receipt confirm ownership identity, authorisation and address authenticity.

The NOC, specifically

A No Objection Certificate is a letter issued by the property owner or coworking operator that says, in effect: “I am okay with you using my property for your business registration.” Think of it as a landlord writing a letter confirming a tenant is allowed to run a business from the rented premises.

Why it matters. Without an NOC, government authorities will reject the company or GST registration application. Missing or incorrectly formatted NOCs are a leading cause of rejection, which makes this one of the most critical documents in the package — and it must be properly signed and stamped.

Sales, payment and refunds

The operational flow

Every Virtual Office sale follows a structured three-step process, and consistency and speed at each stage directly affect conversion.

Step 1 — the lead arrives on Zoho CRM. Leads come from paid ads, social media, SEO or the website, and are auto-assigned to a sales agent with name, email, mobile, city and space preference.

Step 2 — contact within 15 minutes. The sooner you call, the higher the conversion rate. Understand the requirement, pitch the right plan, and listen more than you speak.

Step 3 — send the proposal and follow up. Send the proposal emailer, update the CRM, set a follow-up date, and keep following up proactively via calls and emails until closure. The proposal itself gives the customer the selected plan, pricing breakup, applicable charges, documentation details, location information, next steps, inclusions, terms and conditions, refund policy and support information — a transparent decision-making document before payment.

How pricing is built

Every plan is priced as base price + payment gateway charges + documentation charges + 18% GST on the total. Discounts apply only on the base price — always clarify this upfront, because a client who assumes the discount applies to the full amount will dispute the final invoice.

Payment routes

Online payment is preferred. It runs through the myHQ website using a payment link you create, coupon codes map instantly, and payment is confirmed immediately with no manual follow-up. Offline or manual invoicing is reserved for large companies that insist on it; payment arrives via NEFT, RTGS or IMPS, and there is typically a 24–48 hour delay before the subscription activates. After payment, fill the VO Transaction Form immediately to ensure accurate billing, onboarding and fulfilment.

Refund policy

A refund is possible before the draft agreement has been signed by the client and approved by the space, or after two government rejections provided the rejection letter is submitted within 60 days of payment. Where eligible, the refund is processed minus a ₹2,000 operational charge.

A refund is not given after the agreement is signed by both parties, if the client does not complete KYC within 30 days and fails to inform us, or if errors in the application were made by the client. Payment gateway charges and documentation charges are never refunded, regardless of the reason.

The process: the client raises a ticket at supportvo@myhq.in with the reason and a cancelled cheque for bank verification; the operations team reviews eligibility within 48–72 hours; approved refunds are credited within 21 working days to the client’s bank account or original payment source.

Fulfilment and KYC

From payment to delivered documents

Fulfilment begins the moment payment is received. The client receives an automatic email with login instructions, logs in at myHQ.in/virtual-office using their payment email ID, and finds their subscription under My Account → Virtual Office → Subscriptions.

From there, one of two paths applies depending on the space — and this expectation needs setting during the sale, not after it.

The ~15 minute path

Upload KYC with business documents and details, review the auto-generated agreement, sign digitally, and receive notarised e-stamp documents. Digital, automated and effectively instant.

The 4–5 working day path

Upload KYC with business documents and details, complete KYC with director and signatory details, the space reviews and approves the KYC, the agreement is generated, signed and uploaded, and the space stamps, notarises and uploads the final documents. This applies to non-WeWork spaces, and the timeline runs from KYC completion rather than from payment.

The 30-day rule. KYC must be completed within 30 days of payment. After this window the client is no longer eligible for a refund under any circumstances.

The specific documents required from the client vary by entity type — Private Limited, LLP, OPC, proprietor or partnership firm, and whether the company is new or already registered. That checklist is covered in detail in the Sales Pitch chapter.

Government verification

What happens after documents are shared

Once clients receive their Virtual Office documents, they apply for company registration via mca.gov.in or GST registration via gst.gov.in. Several things may follow.

A government clarification notice may ask for more documents, which the client uploads via the dashboard or raises as a support ticket. An on-ground verification may see officials visit the space to verify documents and photograph signage — the space handles this, and in most cases no action is needed from the client. Physical verification requires a company representative to be present; neither myHQ nor the space can impersonate the client, and this must be communicated clearly during the sales process rather than discovered on the day. Throughout the year, mail and packages arrive at the space for collection in person or courier delivery.

What changed in GST 2.0

Registrations are no longer approved on basic document submission alone. Verification is now risk-based and validation-driven, taking into account the nature of the business, address authenticity, the applicant’s profile and filing behaviour, and document consistency and digital validation. There is no fixed scrutiny checklist — verification depends on the authority’s risk assessment.

What authorities check, at each level

Customer level: company registration details are verified with MCA, business activity and projected turnover are reviewed for GST eligibility, the authorised signatory’s identity and authority are confirmed, and understanding of GST obligations — filing returns, paying taxes, maintaining records — is assessed.

Space level: the legitimacy of the coworking space is verified via utility bills and property agreements, subleasing authorisation is confirmed as properly documented, the NOC from the property owner must be in place, and for leased spaces the Master Agreement between space and building owner is validated.

How different spaces handle verification

WeWork spaces use a fully system-based approach. No physical signage is displayed; WeWork uses open areas, phone booths and meeting rooms to represent Virtual Office companies during government verification. Auto-approval gives a faster turnaround — a minimum of 15 minutes post-KYC in most cases.

Non-WeWork spaces display signage at reception showing company names and GST numbers, reserve designated areas or floors for Virtual Office companies, and during GST verification drives authorities verify active legal agreements rather than requiring physical occupancy. The timeline is 4–5 working days post-KYC completion.

Both approaches are government-accepted. What matters is that documents are complete, the address is active, and signage — where required — is properly displayed before any verification visit.

Geo-tagged images and bank account verification

Clients can request geo-tagged images of the location, signage or verification setup when required for GST or banking compliance. Raise a ticket at supportvo@myhq.in with the client name, subscription ID and the specific image requirement; the ops team coordinates with the space, subject to partner availability.

For bank account opening, banks commonly require the Certificate of Incorporation, company PAN, GST certificate where available, MOA and AOA, a board resolution for account opening, director PAN and Aadhaar, and office address proof — which for a Virtual Office means the agreement, NOC and utility bill. Verification may be document-based, digital (MCA records, GST check, website presence, email and phone validation), or physical and video-based (video KYC, geo-tagged office images, signage verification, and in some cases a physical visit).
