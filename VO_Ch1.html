2.2 · Empathy & customer first

Understand before you respond.

Empathy here is not softness. It is the ability to read customer context, concerns and intent before acting — which is what makes the eventual recommendation land.

Empathy in practice
  • Listen to understand. Listen completely before responding, assuming or offering solutions.
  • Objections are signals. Concerns usually indicate gaps in clarity, confidence or trust — not resistance.
  • Context before action. Better decisions and smoother experiences come from acting with full context.
Customer first in practice
  • Solve, don’t sell. The right solution is always better than the fastest close.
  • Clarity over urgency. A customer who understands what they are buying stays longer and refers others.
  • Everyone’s responsibility. Customer experience is a standard, not a department.
The filter. Every recommendation must pass one test — is this right for the customer? If it is not right for them, it is not right for us.

Culture and conduct

The six pillars

myHQ Virtual Office runs on six cultural pillars. They are not aspirational language — they describe the behaviours expected in every interaction, and they are what performance conversations actually measure.

PillarWhat it means in practice
DisciplineConsistent execution every day, without reminders.
OwnershipIf it is yours, it gets done completely, correctly and on time.
Customer firstEvery decision is guided by what is right for the customer.
Mutual respectWe win as one team. Every function matters.
EmpathyUnderstand fully before you respond or act.
ResilienceStay consistent through rejection, pressure and uncertainty.

Discipline

Discipline is not about control. It is about professionalism, accountability and predictable execution — being someone colleagues and customers can rely on without having to check. In practice it means logging in on Keka by 10:00 AM, delivering consistently on quality and punctuality, planning leaves with proper business continuity alignment, maintaining professional communication at all times, following defined sales and operational processes without exception, and being dependable in both customer handling and internal teamwork.

Attendance policy: three regularisations are allowed. Subsequent instances are marked as Loss of Pay (LOP).

Empathy

Empathy at myHQ is not softness. It is the ability to understand customer context, emotions, concerns and intent before responding or acting. Three habits make it real: listen completely before responding or offering solutions; treat objections as signals rather than resistance, because they usually indicate a gap in clarity, confidence or trust; and take action only with full context.

Customer first

We do not push products. We enable better decisions. Every recommendation must pass one test: is this right for the customer? Solve rather than sell — the right solution is always better than the fastest close. Prioritise clarity over urgency, because a customer who understands what they are buying stays longer and refers others. And treat customer experience as a standard rather than a department: every touchpoint, whether a call, an email or a handover, shapes how the customer feels about myHQ.

Ownership

Ownership is not just completing a task — it means owning the outcome and finding a way to make it happen. 100% responsibility is the baseline, not the achievement. Complete the task, own the outcome, drive the solution; never stop at “done”. Anticipate blockers before they escalate, question whether the process could be better, and never just report a problem — come with a solution or a clear way forward.

True ownership looks like: no reminders needed, proactive communication, end-to-end accountability, reliable commitments and continuous improvement.

Resilience

Challenges are constant; performance is defined by your response to them, not by their absence. A “no” is data, not defeat. Continue follow-ups without losing energy or changing your professional tone, recalibrate quickly after difficult conversations, and trust that consistency compounds — small disciplined actions create large outcomes over time.

Mutual respect

Ops, BD, Product, Marketing, Finance and Sales all contribute to the same customer journey and the same business outcome. No function is secondary. Respect shows up as professional communication, timely responses, accountability and clarity in execution. If teams are aligned internally, customers experience consistency externally.

If execution is inconsistent, performance cannot scale. The pillars exist because Virtual Office is a high-volume, compliance-sensitive business where one unreliable link creates downstream problems for the customer and the team.
