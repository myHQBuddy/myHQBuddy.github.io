05

Registration concepts

What the client is actually trying to do

You do not file these on the client’s behalf, but you will be asked about them on nearly every call. Knowing the basics is the difference between a confident answer and a lost deal.

Company registration

Company registration is the process of legally creating a business under Indian law through the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. It is like giving the business a birth certificate: after it, the company officially exists in the eyes of the law as a separate legal ‘person’ that can own things, sign contracts, and shield the owner’s personal assets if the business faces losses or lawsuits.

The benefits a client cares about are legal protection for personal assets, credibility with clients, banks and investors, easier access to funding, and eligibility for various government tax deductions.

The process on mca.gov.in: choose the business structure (Pvt. Ltd., LLP, OPC and so on), obtain a Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) for online filing, obtain a Director Identification Number (DIN) for all directors, file the RUN form for name approval, then the SPICe form with MOA and AOA, and finally receive the Certificate of Incorporation from the Registrar of Companies.

GST registration

GST registration is how a business gets officially recognised as a tax-paying entity — effectively a tax ID card for the company. It is mandatory if annual turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for North-Eastern states), and required for inter-state trade and all e-commerce businesses. Registration allows the business to claim Input Tax Credit on its purchases.

The process on gst.gov.in: create a user ID, fill form GST REG-01 with business details, upload documents (PAN, Aadhaar, address proof, bank details), receive an ARN as a tracking number after submission, and receive the GSTIN on successful verification.

Mailing address and official correspondence

The third concept is the simplest. A business’s official correspondence address is the one that appears on its website, letterhead, visiting card and Google Business profile. It does not imply the business operates from there. Clients, banks and suppliers send important letters to it, and with a Business Address plan that premium address belongs to the client — all letters and packages are received and stored safely at the space, for collection in person or courier delivery on request.

06

The document chain

Why the paperwork exists

The document chain is what legally establishes a client’s right to use a commercial address. It runs in three links: the landlord, who owns the property; the space operator, who leases, manages and provides the workspace; and the client’s business, which uses the address for registration, compliance and correspondence.

From landlord to operator flow the lease or rent agreement, ownership and property proof, an NOC where applicable, and other supporting property documents. From operator to client flow the agreement or service contract, authorisation to use the address, address-related documents and business-specific documentation.

Owned versus leased space

An owned space means the coworking operator owns the building. A leased space means they rent it from someone else. The distinction matters because the documents required for a Virtual Office application differ: for an owned space, documents come directly from the space; for a leased space, the operator must first have obtained the building owner’s permission, which requires a Master Agreement and a Master NOC to prove the chain of permission.

Recommendation: owned spaces are preferred. They are more stable, have lower rejection rates and require fewer supporting documents. When a client has flexibility on location, guide them towards owned-space options.

What spaces provide

A space–client agreement is the legal contract authorising the client to use the address. The NOC grants official permission to use the address for government registration. A utility bill — electricity or water — confirms the physical address is real and active. For leased spaces only, a Master Agreement establishes the operator’s right to sublease and a Master NOC records the building owner’s permission to sublease to multiple clients. Supporting documents such as landlord KYC, board resolution and property tax receipt confirm ownership identity, authorisation and address authenticity.

The NOC, specifically

A No Objection Certificate is a letter issued by the property owner or coworking operator that says, in effect: “I am okay with you using my property for your business registration.” Think of it as a landlord writing a letter confirming a tenant is allowed to run a business from the rented premises.

Why it matters. Without an NOC, government authorities will reject the company or GST registration application. Missing or incorrectly formatted NOCs are a leading cause of rejection, which makes this one of the most critical documents in the package — and it must be properly signed and stamped.

The full plan-by-plan document matrix — which documents come with which plan, and what the client must provide for each entity type — is covered in the Sales Pitch chapter.
